**4.1 From internal watchdogs to independent watchdogs** – Meg Mitchell and Timnit Gebru first institutionalised “ethics-by-documentation” at Google through Model Cards and Datasheets for Datasets. After their public refusal to withdraw a critical paper on large-language-model harms led to their dismissal, they co-founded the not-for-profit Distributed AI Research Institute (DAIR), signalling a shift from corporate compliance work to fully independent, community-rooted inquiry.

**4.2 DAIR’s research philosophy underpins \*Measuring Diversity\*** – Podcast guests Alex Hanna, Dylan Baker and Milagros Miceli describe DAIR’s guiding principles: AI is not inevitable, its harms are preventable, and research must be non-extractive and accountable to affected communities.  *Measuring Diversity* inherits this stance by turning value-laden choices (e.g., utilitarian vs maximin aggregation) into visible “knobs” that stakeholders can debate rather than accept by default.

**4.3 Classification as a site of power** – The episode stresses that “classification is never neutral”; every label naturalises a worldview and can reinforce global North/South, race, or gender hierarchies.  The Presence/Inclusion metrics explicitly guard against such tokenistic “counting” by asking both **who appears** and for whom the set is meaningful, echoing DAIR’s critique of hidden epistemic authority.

**4.4 Participatory, worker-led knowledge production** – DAIR funds projects where data workers and refugee advocates define research questions and own the narrative, rejecting the parachute-research model.  In the same spirit, the *Measuring Diversity* Colab and Explorable are open-source tools meant to be forked, localised, and governed by the very communities whose representation they score.
